The problem

Beautiful reports. Zero readers.

The team's Monday report was a work of art — twelve slides, hand-built, six hours of an analyst's week. The open rate said 11%. The data was fine; the format was a monologue. What the team actually needed was the three things that changed since last week, ranked by what they'd cost to ignore.

The constraint that shaped everything: no new habit allowed. If it required anyone to open a tool they didn't already open, it would fail exactly like the PDF did.

Constraints & decisions

Where AI paid — and where rules won.

Rules for arithmetic, LLM for language→ Numbers computed deterministically; the model only narrates what changed and why it matters. No hallucinated metrics.
Push, don't pull→ Monday 9:00 summary into WhatsApp + email — channels the team already lived in. The dashboard became optional.
Signals over charts→ Three card types — risk, pattern, forecast — each with a next action attached. Nothing arrives without a suggestion.
Human review gate→ Anything customer-facing drafts as an email, never sends itself. Automation earns trust before autonomy.
What shipped

A signal layer with a voice.

The pipeline runs nightly: deterministic statistics ( thresholds, deltas, cohort math) feed an LLM step that writes the one-paragraph "what changed and what to do" — capped, grounded in the numbers it was handed, and cheap to run at this volume. Weekly digest assembles itself; drafts queue for review; nothing customer-facing leaves without a human click.

Outcome & the honest retro

The report nobody read became the message everybody answers.

The Monday digest gets replied to in-thread — the team argues with the signal, which is exactly the point. The Tuesday +22% order pattern was found in week two and had been sitting in the data for a year. Three churn risks surfaced before renewal dates, two saved. The analyst got her six hours back and spent them on the cohort study that found the third.

What I'd do differently: I'd start with the digest alone — dashboard second. The push channel proved the value in ten days; the dashboard took three more weeks and is used by exactly two people. Also: log every generated sentence with its source numbers from day one. It made every "is this right?" conversation a thirty-second check instead of a debate.
