The problem

"Reconnecting…" was the most-read sentence in the app.

The crews' territory included basements, rural sites, and lifts — places where connectivity is theoretical. The old app submitted job reports through a live POST. When the request failed, the report failed; crews either re-typed it at the office or skipped digital entirely and kept paper duplicates for safety. Dispatch reconciled the two systems every evening, by hand.

The crews stopped carrying paper on day one. That's the whole review. — Operations lead, logistics company
Constraints & decisions

Offline isn't a mode. It's the terrain.

Local-first, always→ Every action writes to on-device SQLite first; the network is an optimization, never a requirement. The app never waits on connectivity.
Queue with idempotency keys→ Outbox pattern with per-report UUIDs — retries can't double-submit, and conflict resolution is last-write-wins with full audit history.
Media syncs separately, throttled→ Job photos compress and upload opportunistically over Wi-Fi; text data (the urgent part) rides the tiny, unreliable pipe first.
Trust the crew, verify the GPS→ One tap opens the pre-filled job card with location and time already attached — data entry becomes data confirmation.
What shipped

A sync engine wearing an app costume.

Flutter for one codebase across the crews' mixed Android fleet; SQLite as the source of truth on device; a server-side reconciliation service that treats every payload as replay-safe. The evening reconciliation ritual ended — dispatch reads the same data the crews wrote, minutes after signal returns.

Outcome & the honest retro

Paper retired. Nobody misses it.

Ninety days in: 98.6% of job reports sync without intervention, the 1.4% that need a retry surface in a crew-visible queue with a reason attached, and admin time per job fell 38% because data arrives structured instead of transcribed. The star rating is the crews', not ours — field workers are the least sentimental reviewers alive.

What I'd do differently: I'd add the "why it didn't sync" queue in v1 instead of v1.2 — the first two weeks of "is it sent?" questions were entirely preventable. Trust in an offline app is built by showing the queue, not by hiding it.
